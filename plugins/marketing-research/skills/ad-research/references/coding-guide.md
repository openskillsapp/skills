# Coding ads

Tag every ad with one primary code per dimension (add a secondary only when
it is clearly there). Consistent codes are what make counts and comparisons
across advertisers possible. Keep the code list below unless the user's
category needs its own; if you add one, define it in the report.

## Angle: the main reason to buy the ad argues

| Code | The ad says... | Example line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pain` | You have this problem and it costs you | "Still chasing invoices on Friday night?" |
| `outcome` | Here is the result you get | "Close your books in 2 hours, not 2 days" |
| `feature` | Here is what the product does | "Auto-match payments to invoices" |
| `social-proof` | Others like you use it | "Join 40,000 teams" |
| `authority` | Experts, press or awards back it | "Rated #1 by Wirecutter" |
| `comparison` | Better than the alternative or a named rival | "Half the price of X" |
| `offer` | A deal is the reason to act now | "40% off this week" |
| `identity` | People like you choose this | "Made for runners who hate shopping" |
| `story` | A founder, customer or origin story | "I built this after my third burnout" |
| `education` | Teaches something, product secondary | "3 mistakes in your first ad account" |
| `objection` | Answers a doubt head on | "No contract. Cancel in one click." |
| `newness` | Launch, new feature, new collection | "Just dropped: the winter range" |

## Hook: how the first line or first seconds stop the scroll

`question`, `bold-claim`, `number` (stat or list), `callout` (names the
audience: "Shopify owners:"), `pattern-break` (odd or funny), `testimonial`
(quote first), `before-after`, `demo` (shows the product in use), `offer-first`.

Quote the hook exactly. For video, use the first line of text or the
caption when the video itself isn't available, and say so.

## Offer

`discount` (with the amount), `free-trial`, `free-shipping`, `bundle`,
`gift`, `guarantee`, `lead-magnet` (guide, quiz, webinar), `demo`,
`none` (pure brand or product ad). Note the exact terms when stated.

## Proof

`numbers` (customers, results), `reviews` (stars, quotes), `logos`,
`press`, `creator` (a person who isn't the brand), `before-after`, `none`.

## Implied audience

Who the ad seems written for, from its words and images: role, life stage,
problem, awareness level (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware,
product-aware, most-aware). Mark this as inference in the report.

## What to count

- Codes across all active ads (the current mix).
- Codes across the survivors (the top 10 by days running). Where the two
  differ, the survivors are the better guide to what works.
- Hooks and offers per landing page, to see how each product is sold.
- For several advertisers: one table of angle share per advertiser.

## Calling patterns

A pattern needs at least 3 ads, or 2 survivors. Name it with its evidence:

> **Pain-first hooks survive.** 5 of their 8 longest-running ads open on a
> pain question (IDs …, …, …), against 30% of active ads overall.

One striking ad is an example, not a pattern. Say which it is.
